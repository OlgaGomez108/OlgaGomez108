## 1. 15-YEAR MARKET CYCLE

### The Three Acts of Spanish Real Estate (2010–2025)

The Spanish real estate market has lived through one of the most dramatic boom-bust-recovery cycles in European history. Understanding this arc is essential for any strategic role in the sector.

```

```

**The three phases:**
- **ACT I — The Crash (2008–2013):** National prices fell **40–43% from peak**. Construction collapsed. Banks accumulated toxic real estate assets. Unemployment hit 26%. Over 500,000 homes sat unsold.
- **ACT II — The Recovery (2014–2019):** Gradual, uneven recovery. Coastal and prime urban areas led; interior cities lagged. Foreign buyers returned first, especially in Málaga, Alicante, Balearics.
- **ACT III — The New Boom (2020–2025):** COVID briefly paused the market but accelerated underlying trends (remote work, lifestyle migration). Post-pandemic surge pushed prices to **new all-time highs** nationally by 2024–2025.

**Critical market insight:** Spain's 2023 affordability crisis was caused by Euribor jumping from –0.5% to +3.9% in under 18 months — a **440bps shock** in less than 2 years. Only ~30% of outstanding mortgages were variable-rate in 2023 (vs. 90%+ pre-2008), limiting the rate shock's transmission to the market.

## 5. MARKET STRUCTURE: AGENCIES & PORTALS

### The Agency Landscape

Spain's real estate agency market is **extremely fragmented** — one of the most fragmented in Europe. There are approximately **100,000 registered real estate agents** nationally, operating through:

- ~50,000+ registered agencies (many micro-businesses)
- Franchise networks (RE/MAX, Century 21, Tecnocasa)
- International luxury brands (Engel & Völkers, Coldwell Banker, Knight Frank)
- Online/hybrid brokerages (Housell, Housfy, Huspy)

**Concentration:** The top 5 franchise networks represent ~8–10% of total transactions. No single company has more than 3–4% market share nationally. **This fragmentation is the key opportunity for platform aggregators like Huspy.**

## 7. THE PROPERTY TRANSACTION PROCESS IN SPAIN

### Complete End-to-End Process

Understanding this process in detail is critical for the Director of Operations role at Huspy, as it maps directly to pipeline management.

```
TIMELINE OVERVIEW

Week 1–2:  [PREPARATION] NIE + Bank Account + Mortgage Pre-approval
Week 2–3:  [SEARCH] Property search via portals + agents
Week 3–4:  [OFFER] Verbal offer → written confirmation
Week 4:    [RESERVATION] Contrato de Reserva (€3,000–€10,000)
Week 4–6:  [DUE DILIGENCE] Legal checks + mortgage application
Week 6–8:  [ARRAS] Contrato de Arras (10% deposit)
Week 8–12: [MORTGAGE] Bank appraisal + final approval
Week 10–14:[NOTARY] Escritura Pública signing + balance payment
Week 14–20:[POST-CLOSING] Tax payment + Registry inscription
```

### Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

**PHASE 1: PREPARATION (1–3 weeks)**
1. **NIE Número de Identificación de Extranjero**
   Required for all foreigners. Applied at Spanish consulate abroad or police station in Spain. Takes 1–4 weeks.
   Cost: ~€10 tax + legal fees if using a lawyer

2. **Spanish bank account**
   Required for payments, tax, utility bills. Most banks open accounts for non-residents.
   Recommended: CaixaBank, Santander, BBVA (large branch networks in Málaga)

3. **Mortgage pre-approval** (if applicable)
   Best practice: Get pre-approval from 2–3 banks before viewing properties.
   LTV available: **70% for non-residents; 80% for residents**
   Required documents: Income proof, employment contract, 2 years' tax returns, bank statements (3 months)

**PHASE 2: SEARCH (ongoing)**
4. Property search via Idealista, Fotocasa + local agents
5. **Critical: foreign buyers often use a buyer's agent** — particularly in Marbella/luxury segment

**PHASE 3: RESERVATION (Week 1–2 from decision)**
6. **Contrato de Reserva**
   Non-binding; deposit of €3,000–€10,000 takes property off market for 2–4 weeks.
   This deposit is refundable if buyer withdraws before signing Arras.

7. **Legal due diligence** (simultaneously):
   - Nota Simple from Land Registry (Registro de la Propiedad) — confirms ownership, mortgage burden, liens
   - Community of owners certificate (no arrears)
   - IBI (council tax) receipts — no arrears
   - Cedula de Habitabilidad (habitation certificate)
   - Energy Performance Certificate (Certificado Energético) — required since 2013
   - Building permits for any extensions/modifications
   - Urban planning confirmation (no nearby infrastructure projects)

**PHASE 4: COMMITMENT — ARRAS CONTRACT (Week 3–5)**
8. **Contrato de Arras Penitenciales** (private purchase contract)
   Legally binding. Buyer pays **10% of purchase price** as deposit.

   | Scenario | Consequence |
   |----------|-------------|
   | Buyer withdraws | Loses 10% deposit |
   | Seller withdraws | Returns **20%** (double the deposit) |
   | Either party fails to complete | Legal action for specific performance |

   Typical timeframe from Arras to Notary: **30–60 days**

**PHASE 5: MORTGAGE FINALIZATION**
9. **Bank appraisal** (Tasación) — €300–€800; takes 5–10 days
10. **Formal mortgage offer** — bank has 10 days to accept/reject once docs submitted
11. **FEIN (Ficha Europea de Información Normalizada)** — standardized mortgage information sheet
12. **Mandatory notarial consultation** — buyer must visit notary alone ≥ 1 day before signing to confirm understanding

**PHASE 6: NOTARY SIGNING — COMPLETION**
13. **Escritura Pública de Compraventa** — all parties present (buyer, seller, bank, lawyers, Gestoría)
    - Remaining balance paid by bank transfer or **cheque bancario (banker's cheque)**
    - Keys exchanged simultaneously
    - Since 2019 Mortgage Act: **bank pays notary costs for mortgage deed** (not buyer)

**PHASE 7: POST-COMPLETION (2–6 weeks)**
14. **Tax payment** — within **30 working days** of signing
15. **Land Registry inscription** — Gestoría files; takes 2–6 weeks
16. **Utility transfers** — electricity, water, gas, community fees

### Rental Regulation Landscape

**2023 Housing Law (Ley de Vivienda)** — major structural change:
- Rent increases capped at **3% in 2024** (for renewals)
- From 2025: new **INE Rental Reference Index** (lower than CPI) as the cap
- "Stressed market zone" designation: municipalities can declare zones where rents are frozen or capped at reference index — Catalonia has done this; Andalusia has been slower
- **Landlord pays agency fees for rental contracts** (reversed prior practice where tenants paid)
- Extended protections for vulnerable tenants (1-year mandatory extension)
- Large landlords (>10 properties) face stricter obligations

**Short-term rental regulation (2025):**
- National vacation rental registry effective **July 1, 2025** — all STR platforms must verify listings
- Málaga city increasingly restricting new tourist apartment licenses in central districts
- Torremolinos has implemented moratoriums in some areas

### Rental Market Supply-Demand Imbalance

**The structural problem:** Spain builds **~95,000 new homes/year** but forms **~246,000 new households/year**. The gap: **~150,000 units of unmet demand annually**. This structural shortage underpins both sale price and rental price growth — and shows **no sign of resolution before 2028–2030**.
