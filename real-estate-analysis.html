Transaction Process

Property Acquisition Through to Sale: End-to-End Process in Spain

A typical transaction takes 10–14 weeks from offer to notary signing. Each stage has distinct legal implications, deposit commitments, and risk-transfer points.

1
Preparation
⏱ 1–3 weeks
Get NIE (€10 tax, 1–4 weeks). Open Spanish bank account. Obtain mortgage pre-approval from 2–3 banks.
LTV: 80% residents / 70% non-residents
2
Search & Offer
⏱ Ongoing
Search Idealista, Fotocasa, agents. Book viewings. Verbal offer → written confirmation. Negotiate 5–10% below asking.
Avg. discount: 6.2% in 2025
3
Reservation
⏱ Week 1–2
Sign Contrato de Reserva. Pay €3,000–€10,000 deposit. Property taken off market 2–4 weeks. Refundable before Arras.
4
Due Diligence
⏱ 2–4 weeks (parallel)
Lawyer checks: Nota Simple (ownership/liens), IBI arrears, community fees, building permits, energy certificate, planning status.
Cost: ~1% of price + 21% VAT
5
Arras Contract
⏱ Week 3–5
Sign Contrato de Arras Penitenciales. Pay 10% deposit. Binding commitment for 30–60 days.
Buyer withdraws → loses 10%. Seller withdraws → returns 20% (double)
6
Mortgage
⏱ Week 6–10
Bank orders Tasación appraisal (€300–€800). Formal offer issued. Mandatory notarial consultation (buyer alone, ≥1 day before signing).
7
Notary Signing
⏱ Week 10–14
Escritura Pública de Compraventa. All parties present. Balance paid by banker's cheque. Keys exchanged. Ownership transfers.
Since 2019: bank pays notary costs for mortgage deed
8
Post-Completion
⏱ Week 14–20
Pay ITP/IVA within 30 days. Gestoría files Land Registry (2–6 weeks). Transfer utilities, community fees, direct debits.
ITP = 7% in Andalusia (Málaga)

Contrato de Arras — Prepay / Pre-Contract ⏱ Week 1–2 after offer accepted
The most critical legal step before closing. Both parties sign the Contrato de Arras Penitenciales — the standard binding pre-contract in Spain. The buyer pays a 10% deposit (señal). If the buyer withdraws: they forfeit the full 10% deposit — it belongs to the seller. If the seller withdraws: they must return double the deposit (20% of agreed price) to the buyer. This is legally enforced under Art. 1454 of the Spanish Civil Code. Timeline to Escritura is fixed here: typically 30–90 days.
Arras Penitenciales — Art. 1454 Civil Code Buyer pays 10% deposit Seller backs out → returns 20% (double) Buyer backs out → loses 10% Private purchase contract (Contrato Privado) 30–90 days to closing fixed at this point
7
Due Diligence & Legal Checks ⏱ 2–4 weeks (parallel with Arras period)
Buyer's lawyer pulls the Nota Simple from the Land Registry to confirm clean title, no liens, no outstanding debts. Seller must clear any existing mortgage (coordination with bank for subrogation or cancellation). Community of owners checked for arrears. Energy Certificate (CEE) must be valid and provided. Any illegal constructions (obras sin licencia) must be declared. If issues found, negotiation re-opens or deal collapses — agent's role is to anticipate and pre-solve.
Nota Simple (Land Registry) Community arrears cert Energy certificate (CEE) Building permit check Mortgage clearance from bank Illegal additions = deal risk
8
Escritura de Compraventa — Notary Closing ⏱ Week 10–14 from offer
Both seller and buyer appear at the notary's office. The Escritura Pública (public deed) is signed. Buyer transfers remaining balance (90%) via banker's cheque or certified bank transfer — all funds must be traceable to comply with anti-money-laundering regulations. Notary reads the deed aloud in full. Keys exchanged. Ownership legally transfers at this moment. Notary registers the change at the Land Registry.
Notary signing Seller receives 90% balance Keys exchanged Escritura Pública signed AML: all funds must be traceable
9
Post-Sale Obligations & Follow-up ⏱ Weeks 14–20 + next tax year
Sale complete — but obligations continue. Seller must pay Plusvalía Municipal (tax on the increase in the cadastral land value, due within 30 days) to the local Ayuntamiento. Capital gain (IRPF) declared in the following year's income tax return. Cancel or transfer utility contracts (electricity, water, gas, internet). Provide buyer with all appliance manuals, community contact, and building documentation. Agent follows up for testimonial, referral, and reinvestment conversation.
Plusvalía Municipal — 30 days to pay IRPF capital gain — next tax year Cancel / transfer utilities Referral & testimonial request Reinvestment conversation: next property?
💡 Huspy opportunity: The buyer pipeline has two high-value Huspy touchpoints — NIE/banking setup (referral partnerships) and mortgage origination (Stage 3). Getting to the buyer at Stage 2–3 means owning the rest of the journey.
1
Define Budget & Goals ⏱ Before anything else
Buyer defines location preferences, property type (apartment, villa, new build vs. resale), intended use (primary residence, holiday home, rental investment), and absolute budget ceiling. Critical: budget must account for 10–12% on top of purchase price for taxes and fees. A €300k property actually requires ~€333k–€336k in total liquidity. Many buyers are caught out by this — agent's first job is to re-frame the true all-in cost.
True cost = price + 10–12% taxes & fees Location shortlist Property type criteria Use case: live / holiday / invest
2
NIE & Spanish Bank Account ⏱ Start immediately — takes 2–3 months
The NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) is a mandatory tax ID for all foreigners buying property in Spain. Without it, no purchase is legally possible — no bank account, no tax payments, no notary signing. Apply at a Spanish police station (Comisaría Nacional) in Spain, or at a Spanish Consulate in your home country. It costs €10 (Tasa 790) but the wait can stretch to 2–3 months in peak season. Open a Spanish bank account simultaneously — all property payments must be made from a Spanish account for AML compliance.
No NIE = cannot complete any purchase NIE — Modelo EX-15 Passport + proof of address Tasa 790 (€10 fee) Open Spanish bank account Start 3+ months before you want to buy
3
Mortgage Pre-Approval ⏱ 2–6 weeks
Before viewing properties, get mortgage pre-approval. Spanish banks lend up to 80% LTV for residents and 70% for non-residents. The bank issues a FEIN (Ficha Europea de Información Normalizada) — the binding European standardised offer — which must be provided at least 10 days before notary signing. Compare at least 3 banks: CaixaBank, BBVA, and Santander dominate the Málaga market. 4
Property Search & Viewings ⏱ Ongoing — typically 1–3 months
Search across Idealista, Fotocasa, Kyero, and Rightmove Overseas. Work with a specialist local agent — in Málaga, having an agent with an international buyer network is critical. Never buy a property unseen: always visit in person. Visit the neighbourhood at different times of day, check proximity to amenities, noise levels, and community condition. Ask specifically about community fees, pending building works, and historical rental performance if it's an investment.
Idealista / Fotocasa / Kyero Rightmove Overseas (UK buyers) Never buy unseen Visit at different times of day Ask: community fees, pending works, rental history
5
Legal Due Diligence (Before Offer) ⏱ 1–2 weeks
Engage an independent Spanish property lawyer (abogado). This is non-negotiable — Spain operates on a "buyer beware" (caveat emptor) model. Your lawyer pulls the Nota Simple from the Land Registry — a €9 document that reveals ownership, any mortgages, liens, easements, and registered size. They also check the Catastro (cadastral record) for planning compliance, query the Ayuntamiento for any outstanding fines or urban planning issues, and verify the energy certificate.
Nota Simple (€9 from Registro de la Propiedad) Catastro check Ayuntamiento planning query Energy certificate (CEE) validation Spain = "buyer beware" — no surprises allowed Illegal constructions = major risk
6
Make an Offer ⏱ 1–5 days to agree
Submit a written offer via the agent. In Málaga's competitive sub-markets (Marbella, Málaga city centre, Benalmádena), low-ball offers are rejected quickly. National average discount from asking price is 6.2% — but Málaga often closes at 2–4% below asking or at asking price for desirable properties. Include proof of funds or mortgage pre-approval letter to signal credibility. In a multiple-offer situation, ask the agent about the process — sealed bids or first-come-first-served.
Written offer via agent Proof of funds / pre-approval letter Málaga avg discount: 2–4% (national: 6.2%) Strong offer = pre-approval attached
7
Contrato de Arras — Binding Pre-Contract ⏱ Within 1–2 weeks of accepted offer
Sign the Contrato de Arras Penitenciales and pay the 10% deposit. The property is immediately taken off the market. This is the point of no return — from here, withdrawing costs you the full deposit. If the seller backs out, they owe you double (20%). The contract fixes the final price, completion date, and any conditions. Having mortgage pre-approval before this stage is essential — Spanish sellers often resist loan-contingency clauses.
Arras Penitenciales — Art. 1454 Civil Code Pay 10% deposit — non-refundable if you withdraw Seller backs out → you receive 20% back Private purchase contract Get pre-approval BEFORE signing Arras
8
Tasación & Mortgage Finalisation ⏱ 3–6 weeks
Bank orders an official property valuation (Tasación) from a certified surveyor (€300–€800). This independently confirms the property's market value — if it comes in lower than the agreed price, the bank lends against the lower figure, potentially requiring the buyer to top up with more cash. Once approved, bank issues the formal FEIN. Mandatory: buyer must visit the notary alone (without the bank) at least 10 days before signing to confirm they understand the mortgage terms — introduced by the 2019 Mortgage Law.
Tasación — €300–€800 FEIN (binding mortgage offer) Tasación < purchase price = cash top-up required Pre-signing notary consultation (mandatory, solo) 10-day mandatory cooling-off after FEIN
9
Escritura de Compraventa — Notary Closing ⏱ Week 10–14 from offer
Final signing at the notary (notario). Buyer and seller (or their legal representatives with Power of Attorney) are present. Full balance paid — typically via banker's cheque (cheque bancario) issued by your Spanish bank. Notary reads the full deed. Both parties sign. Keys change hands. Legal ownership transfers at this precise moment. Since 2019, the bank pays its own notary and registry costs for the mortgage deed — buyer no longer bears these.
Notary signing — both parties present Pay remaining 90% via banker's cheque Keys received Escritura Pública signed Bank pays its own notary costs (since 2019)
10
Registration, Taxes & Post-Purchase Setup ⏱ Weeks 14–20
Pay ITP (7% in Andalusia for resale) or IVA + AJD (10% + 1.2% for new build) within 30 days of signing — your gestoría typically handles this. Land Registry transfer takes 15–30 working days. Transfer utilities (electricity: Endesa/Iberdrola, water: EMASA in Málaga), set up community fee direct debit. If investment property: register with local tourist authority for rental licence (VFT, if applicable). Agent follows up with utility transfer checklists and local service introductions.

Essential Spanish Real Estate Terminology

These are the terms every Huspy agent must know fluently — they come up at every stage of both pipelines.

Arras Penitenciales
Binding deposit contract
Standard 10% deposit pre-contract. Buyer loses it if they withdraw; seller pays double if they withdraw. Governed by Art. 1454 Civil Code.
Escritura de Compraventa
Public deed of sale / Closing
The notarised title deed signed at closing. Legal ownership transfers at the moment of signing.
Nota Simple
Land Registry extract
A €9 document from the Registro de la Propiedad showing current owner, mortgages, liens, and registered description. First thing any lawyer checks.
Tasación
Official bank valuation
Mandatory certified appraisal ordered by the bank (€300–€800). Sets the maximum loan amount — if lower than purchase price, buyer must top up in cash.
Plusvalía Municipal
Municipal capital gains tax (seller)
Tax on the increase in the cadastral land value since the last sale. Always paid by the seller — due within 30 days at the local Ayuntamiento.
NIE
Foreigner ID number
Número de Identificación de Extranjero. Mandatory for all foreigners buying property, opening a bank account, or paying taxes in Spain. Takes 2–3 months to obtain.
FEIN
European mortgage offer sheet
Ficha Europea de Información Normalizada. Binding mortgage offer — must be given at least 10 days before notary signing (2019 Mortgage Law).
ITP
Property Transfer Tax (buyer)
Impuesto de Transmisiones Patrimoniales. Paid by buyer on resale properties. 7% in Andalusia — one of Spain's lowest rates.
Mandato de Exclusividad
Exclusive listing agreement
Agreement granting one agency sole rights to sell. Delivers faster sales and better marketing investment vs. open mandates with multiple agencies.
